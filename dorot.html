The Product

Four stages. Birth to adulthood. One continuous thread.

Dorot follows the child , not the Jewish calendar, not the school year. Four developmental stages from 0 to 18, each with a distinct product language matched to how children actually form identity, language, belonging, and meaning at that age.

Stage 1 · Ages 0,3
Bereishit
בְּרֵאשִׁית , "In the Beginning"

The sensory foundation. Jewish culture arrives through touch, smell, and rhythm before language. Shabbat mobile, havdalah spice rattle, soft challah, high-contrast Hebrew board books. The holidays become first memory, not lesson.

Sensory Ritual Objects Hebrew Sounds Shabbat
Stage 2 · Ages 3,7
Sippur
סִפּוּר , "Story"

The narrative stage. Children this age are wired for story, character, and moral logic , exactly what Jewish tradition is built from. Torah portion illustrated storybooks, seder plate pretend-play, Purim costume kit, tzedakah box and giving rituals.

Storytelling Holidays Values Pretend Play
Stage 3 · Ages 7,13
Chaver
חָבֵר , "Friend / Member"

The belonging stage. Jewish identity becomes social , belonging to a people, a history, a land. Hebrew language tools, Jewish history timelines, Israel & diaspora map kit, ethical debate cards (Talmudic style), and bar/bat mitzvah preparation materials.

Hebrew History Ethics Community
Stage 4 · Ages 13,18
Daat
דַּעַת , "Knowledge / Becoming"

The identity stage. Post-bar mitzvah, Jewish identity either deepens or dissolves. Dorot's final stage meets teenagers where they are: contemporary Jewish thought, Israel complexity, modern antisemitism, and the question every generation must answer , what does it mean to be Jewish today?

The Philosophy

Identity is formed in the first 2,000 days. Not at bar mitzvah.

Dorot is built on the convergence of developmental science and 3,000 years of Jewish wisdom about how culture survives. The Haggadah didn't last this long by accident.

◎
Developmental windows are non-negotiable

The brain is most plastic in years 0,3. Language, identity, and emotional memory are laid down during this window , not during Hebrew school. Dorot meets children where the science says to meet them.

✦
Judaism's genius is its objects and rituals

Jewish tradition is a multi-sensory technology. Challah, spice boxes, Hanukkah candles, the seder plate , they encode memory in smell, touch, taste, and rhythm. Dorot weaponizes this deliberately.

▲
For secular families first, religious families always

Dorot is designed for the majority , the culturally Jewish, unaffiliated, interfaith family that wants their kid to know who they are. No guilt. No obligation. Just beautiful, intelligent connection.

⬡
Parents learn too , that's the product

Dorot kits come with a beautifully-designed parent guide. Short. Modern. Contextual. Many American Jewish parents know they want to pass something on , they just don't know exactly what or how. Dorot teaches both generations at once.
